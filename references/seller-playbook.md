# Seller playbook

Use during brief, hypotheses and thumbnail review. These are starting hypotheses, not measured universal effects or marketplace requirements.

## Feed-first heuristics

- Hypothesis: a shopper decides in less than a second; evaluate a 180 px tile before the full-size image.
- Hypothesis: 70–85% product occupancy and one focal message improve recognition; category rules can override this range.
- Hypothesis: contrast with the category's usual background can attract attention. If most competitors use white, test a different permitted background; if crowded/colored, test quiet white. Do not confuse bright clutter with contrast. WB cautions against excessive graphics.
- Hypothesis: at most one verified USP plus one factual badge remains readable. This is a ceiling, not a requirement to add either.
- Suspected CTR problems: small product, dense copy, stock-photo appearance. Suspected CR/return problems: imagery implies a different color, dimensions, contents or result than delivered.
- A claim about a return-rate effect needs actual return data; orders alone cannot prove it.

## Category hypotheses

| Category | Candidate one-variable test | Product-truth check |
|---|---|---|
| Apparel | Product scale in an existing full-height model photograph; or light alone | Do not generate a new fit, fabric, length or body-product interaction; new pose needs a real source |
| Electronics | One verified specification versus the same image without that text | Model, ports, logo and included accessories unchanged |
| Cosmetics | Verified texture detail in the composition, using a real reference | No invented efficacy, before/after result or changed shade |
| Home | Background contextualized in an interior | No false scale, extra supplied furniture or changed product perspective |
| Children | One real scale cue versus no cue | Accurate dimensions; no implied age/safety certification |
| Food | A single factual composition/weight USP | Packaging, ingredients, portion and weight accurate; no invented health claim |

These category patterns may require a new real photograph rather than generative editing. If the requested variable cannot be isolated, reject the candidate, not the one-variable rule.

## Hypothesis record

Produce 4–6 Russian hypotheses from brief, applicable patterns, available competitor screenshots and `experiments/learnings.md`. Do not invent competitor observations when screenshots are absent.
Each has a unique id, one `variable`, exact `change` vs control, contextual `rationale`, `target_metric`, ICE impact/confidence/ease 1–5, and marketplace/product-distortion `risks`.
ICE is a prioritization judgment, not a measured prediction; rank by the mean, disclose that confidence reflects evidence. Select 1–2 challengers, or exactly one for native WB. Generate 2–3 candidates per selected hypothesis; pick one approved candidate per arm, never treat all candidates as test arms.

## Pre-test checklist

1. Real reference and current control belong to this exact SKU/variant.
2. Color, shape, logo, packaging and kit contents match the delivered product.
3. Exactly one intended variable differs; unintended changes were rejected.
4. Product and factual text are readable at 180 px in a two-column feed.
5. Category-specific marketplace rules are verified, not assumed from this playbook.
6. One approved file per challenger; control processing is consistent with challengers.
7. Price, advertising and stock held stable; no known promotion/holiday at start.
8. Primary outcome, MDE, allocation, duration and stopping conditions frozen before exposure.
9. Variant-specific metrics share attribution/units; repeats/clustering and order delay understood.
10. Control retained for rollback; actual cabinet launch/upload remains the seller's action.
